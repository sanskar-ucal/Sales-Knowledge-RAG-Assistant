# MEDDICC Qualification Framework

MEDDICC is a qualification framework used in complex B2B sales. It helps reps determine whether an opportunity is real, winnable, and worth investing time in. MEDDICC is an acronym for Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion, and Competition. The original framework, MEDDIC, did not include the final "C" for Competition.

## Metrics

Metrics are the quantifiable business outcomes the customer expects from the solution, such as reducing onboarding time by 30% or increasing pipeline coverage. Metrics should be stated in the customer's own numbers and validated with them. Without metrics, it is hard to justify the purchase internally.

## Economic Buyer

The Economic Buyer is the person with the authority to approve the spend and who can say yes when everyone else says no. This is usually not the day-to-day user. Reps should aim to meet the Economic Buyer directly before the proposal stage and understand what that person personally cares about.

## Decision Criteria

Decision Criteria are the technical, business, and commercial requirements the customer will use to compare options. Examples include security certifications, integrations, total cost of ownership, and time to value. Good reps try to shape decision criteria early so they favor their solution's strengths.

## Decision Process

The Decision Process describes the steps, people, and timeline involved in making the decision: who evaluates, who approves, legal and security review, procurement, and signature. Map the decision process together with the champion and write down the date of each step.

## Identify Pain

Pain is the business problem that makes the status quo unacceptable. Strong pain is tied to a cost, a risk, or a missed goal, and has a clear owner. If there is no pain, there is no urgency and the deal will likely stall.

## Champion

A Champion is a person inside the customer organization who has power and influence, has a personal interest in your success, and actively sells on your behalf when you are not in the room. Test a champion by asking them to do something, such as arranging a meeting with the Economic Buyer. A friendly contact who will not take action is a coach, not a champion.

## Competition

Competition includes other vendors, internal build options, and the choice to do nothing. Doing nothing is frequently the most common competitor. Understand which alternatives the customer is evaluating and how they compare against the decision criteria.

## Using MEDDICC in deal reviews

During pipeline reviews, managers score each MEDDICC element as green (validated), yellow (partially known), or red (unknown). Any opportunity in the commit forecast category should have the Economic Buyer, Decision Process, and Champion validated. Red elements should translate into specific next actions for the rep.
