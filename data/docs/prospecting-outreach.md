# Prospecting and Outbound Outreach

## Ideal Customer Profile

The Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) describes the type of company that gets the most value from the product and is most likely to buy. An ICP typically includes industry, company size, geography, technology stack, and a triggering event. Prospect only into accounts that match the ICP; low-fit accounts have lower win rates and higher churn.

Buyer personas complement the ICP by describing the individual roles involved in a purchase, such as the economic buyer, the technical evaluator, and the end user.

## Multi-touch sequences

A standard outbound sequence includes 8 to 12 touches over 3 to 4 weeks across multiple channels: email, phone, and LinkedIn. Most meetings are booked after the fourth touch, so stopping after one or two attempts leaves meetings on the table.

Example 3-week sequence:
- Day 1: personalized email and LinkedIn connection request
- Day 3: phone call and voicemail
- Day 5: follow-up email with a relevant case study
- Day 8: phone call
- Day 10: LinkedIn message referencing a recent company event
- Day 14: email with a short insight or benchmark
- Day 17: phone call
- Day 21: break-up email asking whether to close the loop

## Writing cold emails

Keep cold emails under 100 words. Lead with something specific to the prospect, state one clear problem you solve, include brief proof, and end with a low-friction call to action such as "Worth a 15-minute conversation next week?" Avoid attachments and multiple links in first-touch emails because they hurt deliverability.

## Cold calling

Open with a permission-based opener, for example "I know I'm calling out of the blue, can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I called, and then you can decide if it's worth continuing?" State a specific problem that similar companies face, then ask a question. The goal of a cold call is to book a meeting, not to sell the product.

## Trigger events

Trigger events are changes that create a reason to buy, such as a new executive hire, a funding round, an acquisition, rapid hiring, or a new regulation. Outreach referencing a trigger event typically gets higher response rates than generic outreach.

## SDR to AE handoff

When an SDR books a meeting, the handoff to the account executive should include the contact's role, the pain or trigger discussed, the qualification notes (at minimum need and timeline), and the meeting agenda. The SDR should attend the first few minutes of the meeting to make introductions.
