# Pricing and Negotiation Guidelines

## Principles of negotiation

Negotiation should start only after the buyer has agreed that the solution solves their problem. Negotiating before value is established turns the conversation into a price-only comparison.

Know your BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement) and try to understand the buyer's BATNA. The party with the stronger alternative has more leverage.

## Give-get trading

Never give a concession without getting something in return. This is called give-get trading. Examples of gets include a longer contract term, upfront annual payment, a signed agreement by a specific date, a case study or reference, or a larger initial commitment.

## Discount approval guidelines

- Discounts up to 10% can be approved by the account executive.
- Discounts between 10% and 20% require sales manager approval.
- Discounts above 20% require VP of Sales approval and a written business justification.
- Multi-year contracts may receive an additional discount of up to 5% per committed year, capped at 15%.

All discounts must be documented in the CRM opportunity with the reason and the corresponding "get".

## Anchoring

Present pricing starting from the most comprehensive package. The first number mentioned in a negotiation tends to anchor the rest of the discussion. Lead with value and the full solution, then let the buyer scale down if needed.

## Handling procurement

Procurement teams are measured on savings, so expect them to ask for a discount regardless of how good the price is. Prepare a small, pre-approved concession for procurement in advance, and make sure the champion and economic buyer have already agreed to the business case before procurement gets involved.

## Walking away

Be willing to walk away from deals where the terms would be unprofitable or where the customer is unlikely to succeed. A bad-fit customer often churns and costs more in support than they pay.
