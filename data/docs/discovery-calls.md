# Discovery Call Playbook

A discovery call is the first substantive conversation with a prospect. Its goal is to understand the prospect's situation, pain, and buying process well enough to decide whether to continue the opportunity. A discovery call is not a product demo.

## Preparing for discovery

Spend 10 to 15 minutes researching before every discovery call. Review the company website, recent news, the prospect's role on LinkedIn, and any prior CRM activity. Write down two or three hypotheses about the problems the prospect is likely facing and the questions that would confirm or reject each hypothesis.

Send a short agenda to the prospect at least 24 hours before the meeting. A good agenda states the purpose of the call, the time box, and the expected outcome, for example "decide together whether a technical evaluation makes sense."

## Structure of a 30-minute discovery call

1. Opening (2-3 minutes): confirm the time available, restate the agenda, and ask whether the prospect wants to add anything.
2. Situation (5 minutes): understand the current process, tools, team size, and who is involved.
3. Pain and impact (10-12 minutes): dig into problems, their business impact, and what happens if nothing changes.
4. Buying process (5 minutes): ask how decisions like this are made, who signs, and what the timeline looks like.
5. Next steps (3-5 minutes): agree on a concrete next meeting with a date, attendees, and purpose before hanging up.

## Asking good discovery questions

Use open-ended questions that begin with "how", "what", or "walk me through". Avoid yes/no questions early in the call. Follow every important answer with at least one layer of "why does that matter?" or "what is the impact of that?" to move from surface symptoms to business impact.

Examples of strong discovery questions:
- "Walk me through how your team handles this today."
- "What happens if this problem is still here in six months?"
- "How are you measuring success for this initiative?"
- "Who else would be affected if you changed this process?"

Aim for the prospect to talk at least 60% of the time. Top-performing reps ask more questions and let silence work for them after asking a question.

## Ending the call

Never end a discovery call without a scheduled next step. A next step is only real if it has a calendar invite, a date, and named attendees. Send a recap email within 24 hours that summarizes the pain points in the prospect's own words, the agreed next step, and any open questions.

## Disqualifying early

It is acceptable, and often desirable, to disqualify an opportunity during discovery. If there is no meaningful pain, no access to a decision maker, or no plausible timeline, politely close the opportunity as "disqualified" in the CRM and offer to reconnect later. Disqualifying early protects forecast accuracy and frees time for better opportunities.
