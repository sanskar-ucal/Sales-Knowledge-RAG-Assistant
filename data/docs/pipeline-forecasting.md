# Pipeline Stages and Forecasting

## Opportunity stages

Every opportunity in the CRM moves through the following stages. Each stage has exit criteria that must be met before the opportunity can advance.

1. Prospecting: a target account has been identified. Exit criteria: a meeting is booked with a relevant contact.
2. Discovery: the first discovery call has happened. Exit criteria: pain is identified and a next step is scheduled.
3. Qualification: the opportunity is evaluated with MEDDICC. Exit criteria: metrics, champion, and decision process are at least partially known.
4. Solution / Evaluation: demos, technical validation, or a proof of concept. Exit criteria: the technical evaluation is successful and the champion confirms you are the preferred vendor.
5. Proposal: pricing and a proposal have been shared. Exit criteria: the economic buyer has reviewed the proposal.
6. Negotiation / Commit: legal, security, and procurement review. Exit criteria: contract signed.
7. Closed Won or Closed Lost.

## Forecast categories

Each opportunity is assigned a forecast category independent of its stage:

- Pipeline: early-stage opportunities with low certainty.
- Best Case: could close this period if things go well, but risks remain.
- Commit: the rep is highly confident it will close this period; the economic buyer is engaged and the paper process is known.
- Closed: already won.

Reps are expected to be accurate in the Commit category within plus or minus 10% each quarter.

## Pipeline coverage

Pipeline coverage is the ratio of open pipeline value to the remaining quota for the period. A healthy target is 3x to 4x coverage at the start of a quarter. Coverage below 3x is an early warning sign that the team will miss the target unless new pipeline is created quickly.

## Sales velocity

Sales velocity measures how fast pipeline turns into revenue. The formula is: number of opportunities x average deal size x win rate, divided by the length of the sales cycle in days. Improving any of the four inputs increases velocity.

## Deal hygiene

Update the close date, amount, next step, and stage after every meaningful customer interaction. An opportunity with a close date in the past or no next step in 14 days is considered stale and should be reviewed with the manager.
