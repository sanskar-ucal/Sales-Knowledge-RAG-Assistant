# Objection Handling Guide

Objections are a normal part of buying. An objection usually signals interest plus a concern that has not been addressed. The goal is not to "win the argument" but to understand the concern and resolve it.

## The LAER method

Use the LAER method for every objection:

1. Listen: let the buyer finish completely without interrupting.
2. Acknowledge: show that you heard and understand the concern, for example "That's a fair concern, and other teams have raised it too."
3. Explore: ask questions to understand the root cause, for example "When you say it's too expensive, what are you comparing it to?"
4. Respond: address the real concern with evidence such as a case study, a calculation, or a trial.

## Common objections and responses

### "It's too expensive"

Price objections are usually value objections. Explore what the buyer is comparing the price against and re-anchor on the cost of the problem identified in discovery. Revisit the metrics: if the solution saves 200 hours per month, compare the price to the cost of those hours. Do not discount immediately; a fast discount signals that the original price was not real.

### "We're already using a competitor"

Ask what they like about the current solution and what they would improve. Focus on the gaps they mention rather than criticizing the competitor. Offer a side-by-side evaluation focused on their decision criteria.

### "Now is not a good time"

Explore what is driving the timing: budget cycle, competing priorities, or lack of urgency. Tie back to the cost of inaction. If the timing truly is wrong, agree on a specific date to reconnect and a trigger event to watch for.

### "Send me some information"

This is often a polite brush-off. Respond by asking what specifically they would like to learn so you can send something relevant, and propose a short follow-up call to review it together.

### "I need to talk to my boss"

Treat this as an opportunity to reach the economic buyer. Offer to help the contact prepare by providing a one-page business case, and ask whether it would be useful for you to join the conversation.

## Handling objections proactively

If the same objection appears in most deals, address it before the buyer raises it. For example, if security reviews often delay deals, share the security documentation in the first technical meeting.
