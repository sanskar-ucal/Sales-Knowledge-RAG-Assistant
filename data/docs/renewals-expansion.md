# Renewals, Churn, and Expansion

## Key retention metrics

- Gross Revenue Retention (GRR): recurring revenue retained from existing customers over a period, excluding expansion. GRR can never exceed 100%.
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR): recurring revenue retained including expansion, minus contraction and churn. NRR above 100% means the existing customer base grows on its own.
- Logo churn: the percentage of customers who did not renew during the period.

A common benchmark for healthy B2B SaaS companies is NRR above 110% and GRR above 90%.

## The renewal timeline

Start the renewal process 120 days before the contract end date for enterprise customers and 90 days for mid-market customers. The renewal motion includes:

1. 120 days out: review product usage, support tickets, and stakeholder changes.
2. 90 days out: hold an executive business review (EBR) to present value delivered against the original metrics.
3. 60 days out: send the renewal proposal.
4. 30 days out: resolve open legal or procurement questions.

## Churn warning signs

Watch for these early signals of churn risk:
- Declining product usage or fewer active users
- The champion or executive sponsor leaves the company
- An increase in escalated support tickets
- The customer stops attending business reviews
- Budget cuts or a merger or acquisition

When a risk signal appears, the account owner should log it in the CRM and create a save plan with specific actions and an executive sponsor from our side.

## Expansion

Expansion revenue comes from upsell (higher tier or more features), cross-sell (additional products), and seat growth. The best time to propose expansion is right after the customer achieves a measurable result, often during an executive business review. Expansion conversations should be grounded in the customer's goals, not in our quota.
